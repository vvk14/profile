Everyone assumes Dawn, Shopify's free default theme, is bad for SEO because it's free. I built a tool to check instead of guessing.

## The Tool

I built a [Shopify Theme Detector](/tools/shopify-theme-detector): paste any Shopify store URL and it tells you the exact theme it's running (name, schema version, whether it's a paid Theme Store theme or a custom build), plus a quick SEO health check, canonical tags, structured data, image alt coverage, and whether the above-fold image is hurting your LCP.

## Method

I ran the tool against 17 real, live Shopify stores across beauty, baby products, and apparel, picking well-known DTC brands rather than cherry-picking ones I expected to score well. Three additional stores I tried were excluded: two blocked the request with bot protection, and one had migrated to a headless storefront with no classic theme data to read.

## Findings

Out of 17 stores checked:

- **Custom vs. Theme Store themes:** only **4 of 17 (24%)** were running an off-the-shelf paid Theme Store theme (Expanse, Split, and two Dawn-based Theme Store listings). The other **13 (76%)** were running fully custom or heavily forked themes.
- **Missing schema:** **14 of 17 (82%)** had no `FAQPage` or `BreadcrumbList` structured data at all, even on stores with large marketing budgets.
- **LCP image handling:** **8 of 17 (47%)** were lazy-loading their above-the-fold hero image, which actively delays Largest Contentful Paint. Almost half.
- **Image alt coverage was the widest spread I saw.** One major beauty brand had alt text on just **6 of 50 images (12%)**. Another had **428 of 471 (91%)**. Same platform, wildly different discipline.

## The Surprising Bit

The 4 paid Theme Store themes in this sample averaged an **8.5/10** score. The 13 custom/forked themes averaged **8.8/10**, slightly *higher*. And the Dawn-based stores specifically (three of them, one running Dawn straight, two running heavily customized forks) averaged **8.3/10**, solidly mid-pack, not the SEO liability people assume.

The real pattern wasn't the theme at all. It was **operational discipline**: stores that generate content fast and touch templates less often (rushed "launch" and promo-named theme versions, several literally titled things like "Fall Homepage" or a launch campaign) tended to skip alt text and schema. The theme is not what tanks a Shopify store's SEO. How consistently the team maintains it is.
